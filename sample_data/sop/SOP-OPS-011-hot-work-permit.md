## 1. Purpose and Scope

This procedure controls any work that produces a flame, spark or heat able to
ignite flammable material — welding, cutting, grinding, brazing, and the use
of non-intrinsically-safe tools — within the refinery battery limits. No hot
work shall start without a valid hot work permit.

## 2. Permit Classes

### 2.1 Class A Hot Work

Class A covers open-flame and spark-producing work in a hazardous area, or on
or near equipment that has contained hydrocarbons. A Class A permit is issued
by the Area Authority, countersigned by the Shift Superintendent, and is valid
for one shift of at most 8 hours.

### 2.2 Class B Hot Work

Class B covers hot work in a designated hot work area outside any hazardous
area, such as the maintenance workshop. A Class B permit is issued by the
Area Authority and is valid for one shift of at most 12 hours.

## 3. Gas Testing

### 3.1 Gas Test Before Hot Work Starts

The Authorised Gas Tester shall test the atmosphere not more than 30 minutes
before hot work starts, at the work point and at every drain, vent or opening
within 15 m. Hot work shall not start unless flammable gas reads 0% of the
lower explosive limit (LEL) at every test point and oxygen is between 19.5%
and 23.5%.

### 3.2 Continuous Gas Monitoring

During Class A hot work a portable gas monitor shall run continuously at the
work point. Work shall stop at once if it reads 5% LEL or more, and shall not
restart until the reading has returned to 0% LEL and the permit has been
revalidated by the Area Authority.

### 3.3 Retesting

The atmosphere shall be retested after any break in hot work longer than
60 minutes, and at least every 2 hours while hot work continues.

## 4. Fire Precautions

### 4.1 Fire Precautions: Fire Watch

A fire watch, trained in the use of the extinguisher provided and holding a
charged 9 kg dry chemical powder extinguisher, shall be present throughout
Class A hot work and for 30 minutes after it ends. The fire watch has no other
duty.

### 4.2 Fire Precautions: Combustibles and Drains

Combustible material within 11 m of the work shall be removed or covered with
fire-resistant blankets, and every drain and sewer opening within 15 m shall
be sealed before hot work starts.

## 5. Hot Work on Process Equipment

### 5.1 Isolation Before Hot Work

Equipment that has contained hydrocarbons shall be positively isolated under
SOP-OPS-015 Clause 2.1, drained, purged or steamed, and gas tested under
Clause 3.1 before hot work on it begins.

### 5.2 Hot Tapping

Welding on equipment that remains in service (hot tapping) is not permitted
under this procedure. It requires a change under SOP-ENG-009 and a written
procedure approved by the Head of Inspection.

### 5.3 Hot Work Inside a Confined Space

Hot work inside a confined space also requires an entry permit under
SOP-OPS-012, and both permits shall be displayed at the entry point.

## 6. Permit Closure

At the end of work the Permit Receiver hands back the permit, and the Area
Authority inspects the site after the fire watch period and signs off. A
permit not closed within its validity lapses; work shall not resume without a
new permit.

## 7. Records and Incidents

Hot work permits are retained for 3 years. Any fire, ignition, gas alarm
during hot work, or breach of a permit condition shall be reported under
SOP-HSE-004 Clause 2.
