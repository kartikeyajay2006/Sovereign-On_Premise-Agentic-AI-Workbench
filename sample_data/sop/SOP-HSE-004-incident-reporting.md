## 1. Purpose and Scope

This procedure applies to every injury, occupational illness, fire, loss of
containment, near miss and unsafe condition on site, whoever is involved,
including contractors and visitors.

## 2. Reporting

### 2.1 Immediate Notification

Any person who witnesses an incident shall make the area safe if they can do
so without risk to themselves, then notify the Shift Superintendent at once by
radio or telephone.

### 2.2 Written Incident Report

A written report shall be entered in the incident register within 24 hours of
the incident, stating what happened, where and when, who was involved, and
the immediate actions taken.

### 2.3 Near Misses

A near miss, an event that could have caused harm but did not, shall be
reported in the same way within 24 hours. Examples include a relief valve
failing its as-received test (SOP-INS-025 Clause 3.3) and an evacuation on a
gas alarm during a confined space entry (SOP-OPS-012 Clause 4.2).

## 3. Incident Classes

### 3.1 Major Incident

A Major incident is a fatality, a lost-time injury, a fire or explosion, or a
loss of containment of 100 kg or more of hydrocarbon, or of any quantity of
toxic material.

### 3.2 Serious Incident

A Serious incident is an injury needing medical treatment beyond first aid,
or a loss of containment of hydrocarbon from 10 kg up to 100 kg.

### 3.3 Minor Incident

A Minor incident is a first aid injury, a loss of containment of hydrocarbon
under 10 kg, or damage to property only.

## 4. Investigation

### 4.1 Investigation of a Major Incident

A Major incident is investigated by a team led by the Plant Manager with the
HSE Manager. The investigation report is due within 30 days of the incident.

### 4.2 Investigation of a Serious Incident

A Serious incident is investigated by a team led by the head of the department
where it occurred. The investigation report is due within 14 days.

### 4.3 Investigation of a Minor Incident or Near Miss

A Minor incident or a near miss is investigated by the supervisor of the area
where it occurred. The investigation is due within 7 days.

### 4.4 Root Causes and Actions

Every investigation of a Major or Serious incident shall identify root causes
with a structured method, such as five-whys or a fault tree, and give every
corrective action an owner and a due date.

## 5. Statutory Notification

Where the law requires an incident to be notified to a statutory authority,
the HSE Manager makes the notification within the period that law sets, and
records its date in the incident register. This procedure does not list those
periods.

## 6. Records

Incident reports and investigations are retained for 10 years. Investigation
reports are classified Sensitive and are readable only by staff cleared for
Sensitive information.
